Assumptions & how this works +

Cost estimate. 100,000 documents × 1,000 input tokens (including questions) × $0.042 per million tokens = $4.20. One pass; excludes retries, refreshes, query interpretation, storage and computation.

Limits. Stored judgments can be wrong or stale. New features require rescoring the archive. These four features complement richer retrieval; they do not replace all the information in a document.

Method. Kieran proposes cosine similarity and is trying it in Cora; his post reports no benchmark. Weighted priorities are our explanatory alternative. Ranking scores are not confidence or accuracy.

Live demo. Jev reads six fixed fictional messages. Results may be cached for one hour. Changing priorities reuses their scores locally.

How we chose the examples

We inventoried 474 distinct catalog URLs across 15 categories in awesome-jev and inspected selected original writeups. Catalogs include experiments, integrations and Jev-inspired alternatives. We do not treat listing counts as adoption. The atlas is a curated selection, not an exhaustive review.

Charts preserve the authors’ sample sizes and limitations. Scenarios are arithmetic on editable assumptions. Emoji examples are authored illustrations. The question playground starts unscored and returns real Jev relevance judgments. The separate feature demo starts with illustrative values and can load real scores. Neither is a benchmark.

A note on the keyboard

Google introduced on-device AI emoji suggestions in 2018. Jev’s opportunity is making contextual features accessible to more builders. This site does not claim that emoji prediction is new.
